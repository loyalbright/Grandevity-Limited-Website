> “You travel far away with the spring wind; I stood beside the river for a long time.”

In just twenty-eight Chinese characters, the Tang-dynasty poet Li Bai gave the world one of its most enduring images of farewell: a single sail fading into the bright distance, and the Yangtze River flowing on toward the horizon.

Grandevity Studio’s **Farewell to Meng Haoran at the Yellow Crane Tower** begins with Li Bai’s complete original poem, then adds newly written Mandarin lyrics—an intimate contemporary chorus and a second verse. The result is a cinematic Mandopop R&B and Neo-Soul ballad about friendship, distance, blessing, and everything that remains unsaid when a dear friend leaves.

**This is not an original-text-only musical setting.** The classical poem and the newly written lyrics are clearly identified below, with selected original lines returning in the bridge and outro.

## 📖 Two Poets and a Farewell in Tang China

Li Bai (701–762), also known as Li Taibai, is celebrated for the imaginative breadth and soaring freedom of his poetry. Yet his farewell poems reveal another side of his art: an ability to express deeply personal feeling through a few carefully chosen images.

The friend in this poem was Meng Haoran (689–740), a distinguished poet from Xiangyang, especially admired for his landscape and pastoral verse. Around twelve years older than Li Bai, Meng was someone whom the younger poet openly respected. In another poem addressed to him, Li Bai begins, “I admire Master Meng, whose grace is known throughout the world.”

The farewell is traditionally associated with the Kaiyuan era of Emperor Xuanzong, although scholars and commentators differ on the precise year. The original Yellow Crane Tower stood near the Yangtze in the Wuchang area of present-day Wuhan, Hubei. Meng Haoran was traveling downstream toward Guangling—the historical name associated here with present-day Yangzhou, Jiangsu.

As his friend’s boat departed, Li Bai turned a familiar scene into a work of extraordinary emotional clarity.

## 🌸 Four Lines, an Endless Horizon

The opening couplet—“My old friend bids farewell to the Yellow Crane Tower in the west; amid the blossoming haze of the third lunar month, he journeys down to Yangzhou”—establishes place, season, and movement. It is spring, not a bleak winter farewell. The riverbanks are alive with colour, suggesting both the beauty of the journey and the ache of separation.

The final two lines shift from movement to stillness. The small sail recedes, becomes a speck, and vanishes into the bright sky. Li Bai remains behind. All that can still be seen is the Yangtze flowing into the distance.

The poem never needs to say, “I cannot bear to let you go.” The duration of the poet’s gaze says it for him. Nor must the “solitary sail” mean that no other vessel existed on the river; it is the only one that matters to the person watching.

## 🎼 A Classical Poem, a Contemporary Mandarin Chorus

This production uses a gentle 6/8 pulse at approximately 74 BPM, combining contemporary Mandopop R&B and Neo-Soul textures with a warm, restrained Mandarin vocal and a spacious instrumental setting.

The new chorus imagines the unsaid words behind Li Bai’s gaze:

> “The words reached my lips, then became a wave; let this whole river of spring water carry you to Yangzhou for me.”

These lines are **newly written lyrics inspired by the poem**, not words preserved from Li Bai or a historical record of the farewell. The second contemporary verse brings a note of blessing and imagined reunion: may the traveler find poetry and wine along the way, and may the two friends climb the tower together again one day.

## 🎞️ The Cinematic MV: From Companionship to an Empty River

Set within an artistically reconstructed Tang-dynasty world, the music video follows Li Bai and Meng Haoran through spring-lit scenes of the Yellow Crane Tower, shared conversation, a farewell on the riverbank, and the departing wooden sailboat. Willow branches, peach blossoms, an expanding river horizon, and the still figure of Li Bai connect the emotional moments.

The film is a poetic historical interpretation rather than an archaeologically exact reconstruction of the Tang-era tower.

## 🌍 English Reading Translation (Not Sung Lyrics)

The English text below is an interpretive reading translation of the **entire song**, including both Li Bai’s poem and the newly written Mandarin lyrics. The recorded performance remains in Mandarin.

**Verse 1 — Li Bai’s original poem**

My old friend bids farewell to the Yellow Crane Tower in the west;  
Amid spring’s blossoming haze, he heads downstream to Yangzhou.  
His solitary sail recedes into the endless blue until it disappears;  
Only the Yangtze remains, flowing where the sky and river meet.

**Chorus — New contemporary lyrics**

With the spring wind, you travel far away;  
I have stood beside the river for so long.  
The words reached my lips, but turned into a wave;  
Let this whole river of spring water carry you to Yangzhou for me.

**Verse 2 — New contemporary lyrics**

The willows along the bank look just as they did before;  
A passing river breeze brushes against my sleeve.  
May poetry and wine accompany you wherever you go;  
When we meet again, let us climb the tower together.

**Bridge — Reprise of the original poem**

His solitary sail recedes into the endless blue until it disappears;  
Only the Yangtze remains, flowing where the sky and river meet.

**Final chorus — Reprise of new contemporary lyrics**

With the spring wind, you travel far away;  
I have stood beside the river for so long.  
The words reached my lips, but turned into a wave;  
Let this whole river of spring water carry you to Yangzhou for me.

**Outro — Reprise of the original poem**

Only the Yangtze remains, flowing where the sky and river meet.
